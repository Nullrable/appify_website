## Cropping Is More Than Trimming Edges

Many people think cropping just means "cutting off what you don't want." In reality, cropping is a tool for **recomposing** — choosing what to include and exclude to emphasize the subject and strengthen the message. Image Converter provides presets for 1:1, 4:3, 16:9, 9:16, and other common ratios, as well as freeform cropping.

## Where the Six Common Ratios Fit

### 1:1 (Square)

- **Typical scenarios**: WeChat avatars, Instagram main images, nine-grid posts.
- **Composition tip**: Center the subject or apply the rule of thirds. Avoid feeling too tight or too empty.
- **Cropping challenge**: Both landscape and portrait photos need recomposing.

### 4:3 (Traditional Monitor)

- **Typical scenarios**: Older photos, PowerPoint illustrations, in-blog imagery.
- **Composition tip**: Horizontal layout with background elements distributed evenly.
- **Cropping challenge**: Hard to keep the subject when cropping from a portrait photo.

### 16:9 (Widescreen)

- **Typical scenarios**: Video covers, banners, desktop wallpapers.
- **Composition tip**: Spread out horizontally. Place the subject in the center or to one side.
- **Cropping challenge**: Portrait photos can keep only a portion of the frame.

### 9:16 (Vertical Video)

- **Typical scenarios**: Douyin / Kuaishou covers, Stories, vertical wallpapers.
- **Composition tip**: Vertical layout with the subject high up or centered.
- **Cropping challenge**: Landscape photos lose a lot of the frame.

### 3:2 (DSLR Photos)

- **Typical scenarios**: Camera-native ratios, photo printing.
- **Composition tip**: Classic photography ratio with flexible subject placement.
- **Cropping challenge**: After cropping, the ratio is flexible, but watch the visual balance.

### 21:9 (Ultrawide)

- **Typical scenarios**: Cinematic frames, ultra-wide wallpapers.
- **Composition tip**: Spread out horizontally to emphasize the sense of environment.
- **Cropping challenge**: Only works with photos that have a clear subject and a wide environment.

## Three Composition Principles

### 1. Subject First

When cropping, decide on the subject first — a face, a product, a key element. Place the subject in the visual center or at a third-line point.

### 2. Breathing Room

- **Too tight**: The frame feels crowded; the subject has no room to breathe.
- **Too empty**: The subject is too small; the frame loses its focus.
- **Just right**: The subject is clear and breathing room guides the eye.

### 3. Visual Balance

As you move the crop frame, check the four corners: are there unwanted elements (passersby, trash cans, utility poles)? Have you broken the symmetry of the original composition?

## Fixed Ratio vs Freeform Cropping

- **Fixed ratio**: Right for standardized output (avatars, covers, platform-fit images).
- **Freeform cropping**: Right for irregular trimming (removing white margins from screenshots, removing background from scanned documents).
