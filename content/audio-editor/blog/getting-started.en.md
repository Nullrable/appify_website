## Getting started

You just opened the audio editor and you're staring at multi-track editing, enhancement, conversion, and extract-audio. Where do you start? This guide walks you through the most common path - take existing material and export a new version.

## Step 1: Open your source

After launching the app you'll see the tool area (Edit / Enhance / Convert / Extract Audio) and a source entry point. Pick an audio file from Photos or Files - any of MP3, WAV, M4A, AAC, FLAC, OGG, Opus, AIFF, AMR, CAF.

If the audio came from a video, head to "Extract Audio" first - pull the audio track out of the video (MOV, MP4, M4V, AVI, MKV, WebM, FLV, WMV) and export it as an audio file, then come back to the editing flow.

## Step 2: Drop it on the timeline

Open the multi-track editor. The clip you picked lands on the timeline automatically - if you picked several, each gets its own track. With a single clip you get one track, but you can also add more material and stack them into one piece.

## Step 3: Trim and rearrange

Each clip has a handle on either end. Drag the start, drag the end, throw away the parts you don't want. You can also split a clip into two at any time point, and edit each half independently. Drag a clip to rearrange order.

## Step 4: Add effects

Each clip can take its own enhancement stack:

- **Noise reduction** - run it first for outdoor recordings
- **Voice enhancement** - common for podcasts and interviews
- **Loudness normalization** - even out multi-clip work where levels differ
- **Channel split / merge / stereo widening** - pick what the material needs

Every effect has a real-time preview - listen until you're happy, then move on.

## Step 5: Speed change / reverse (optional)

Need a different speed or reverse? Operate it directly on the track. A continuous slider from 0.25× to 4×; reverse is one-tap.

## Step 6: Preview and export

Before exporting, listen to the whole thing inside the app - what you hear should match what you'll get out. Once it sounds right, hit export.

## Step 7: Save

After export, the new file is first written to the app's sandbox folder, then a prompt asks you to pick "Save to Files". Choose a folder inside the iOS Files app, and the new file lands there.

The original source is never modified - keep the master if you want.
