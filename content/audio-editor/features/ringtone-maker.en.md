## Ringtone maker

iPhone ringtones cap at 30 seconds (the system allows up to 40, but staying under 30 keeps the sound tight). The file format is M4R, and the source comes from local files synced through iTunes or Finder. Turning a favorite song, podcast quote, or a kid's recording into an iPhone ringtone is a short workflow.

## Picking the start and end

Once your clip is loaded, drag the two handles on the timeline to set the in-point and out-point:

- **In-point** — where the ringtone begins. Most clips skip the intro or transition before the chorus
- **Out-point** — where the ringtone ends. Under 30 seconds stays punchy; chorus or a single quote usually fits
- **Preview** — listen through once in-app to confirm the cut feels right

The source file is trimmed inside the app sandbox; the original is never touched.

## Fade in and fade out

If you don't want a hard cut at the start or end, layer in fades:

- **Fade in** — rises from silence, adjustable from a few seconds up to ten-plus
- **Fade out** — ends naturally instead of "popping" off

Fades are optional. Some material - heavy drums, chorus that drops straight in - actually sounds worse with a fade.

## Loudness normalization

Quiet source material risks being missed when the phone rings. Normalization pulls loudness up to a consistent level, so the ringtone won't sound weak regardless of how soft the original was.

## Exporting M4R

Once you're happy, export as M4R. M4R is the iOS-only ringtone container - essentially AAC audio with a ringtone flag. Small file size, broad compatibility.

After export:

1. **Mac users** - drag the .m4r file into Finder's "Tones" tab for your iPhone, or drop it into the Music app's ringtone library
2. **Windows users** - drag the .m4r file into iTunes' "Tones" library
3. Once synced, the new ringtone appears under "**Settings → Sounds & Haptics → Ringtone**" on your iPhone

## Source material that works

- Pop song chorus or hook
- Podcast quote
- Your own catchphrase
- A kid's recording
- Classic movie line
- Favorite game sound effect

## Pairing with enhancement

If the original is too quiet, noisy, or has overly wide stereo, run it through the enhancement tools first:

- Outdoor recordings get denoised first
- Inconsistent loudness gets normalized
- Over-compressed stereo gets widened slightly

Enhancement isn't required for ringtone-making - a clean source often needs nothing else.

## Fully local

Trimming, fades, normalization, M4R encoding - all run on the iPhone. Source isn't uploaded, intermediate files don't sit on a server.
