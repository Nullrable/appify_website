## Extract audio from video

A video has a line of dialogue, a song, a recording you want to keep, but you only want the sound. Toss the picture, keep the track, drop it into earbuds, podcasts, or your next edit - lighter than carrying the video with you.

## Supported video sources

Eight containers are covered: **MP4, MOV, M4V, AVI, MKV, WebM, FLV, WMV**. The editor itself never opens video; it only treats video as an "audio source."

## Audio output formats

Once the track is pulled, pick one of four formats as the container: **MP3, WAV, FLAC, OGG**.

| Format | Best for |
|---|---|
| **MP3** | The universal one - supported by virtually every device and player |
| **WAV** | Uncompressed master, ideal for further editing or archival |
| **FLAC** | Lossless compression, about half the size of WAV |
| **OGG** | Open-source container, preferred by some professional gear |

## How to use

Pick one or more videos from Photos or Files. After selection, a format chip appears at the top. Pick MP3 for general use, WAV to keep as a master, FLAC when you want lossless but find WAV too large - whatever the situation calls for.

## How processing works

Pick one or more videos at once, and the audio track is pulled from each. The original video stays untouched - what comes out is a new file, and which one you keep is up to you.

## Where output lands

When done, the audio file is first written to the app's sandbox directory (named after the source video with the new extension), then a prompt asks you to "Save to Files" so the iOS Files app can place it in your chosen folder. The extracted MP3/WAV/FLAC/OGG doesn't go into Photos directly. The original video isn't touched.

## Fully on-device

Video and audio files stay on your iPhone the whole time - never uploaded, never routed through any server. Even if you're underground with no signal, exporting still works.
