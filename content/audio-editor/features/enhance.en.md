## Audio enhancement

Recordings, podcasts, interview material, outdoor audio - raw source almost always has problems: floor noise, reverb, inconsistent loudness, a cramped soundstage. The enhancement toolkit cleans these up one by one, without touching the content itself.

## Six actions

- **Channel split** - Pull stereo apart into two mono tracks for separate editing
- **Channel merge** - Combine two mono tracks into one stereo track, or one dual-mono
- **Noise reduction** - Tame persistent floor noise (air conditioning, wind, hum) while keeping transients
- **Voice enhance** - Bring out vocal frequencies, soften the backing or ambient sound
- **Normalize** - Pull clips with inconsistent loudness to a common level
- **Stereo widening** - Open up the soundstage so a narrow recording feels more spacious

## Stacking them

These actions aren't toggles, they're modules. You can stack several on the same clip, in any order. Common chains:

- Outdoor interview: Noise reduction → Voice enhance → Normalize
- Old cassette transfer: Noise reduction → Stereo widening → Normalize
- Podcast edit: Channel split (guest on its own track) → Noise reduction → Normalize → Channel merge

The order changes the result; drag them around and listen to find the chain that suits your source.

## How enhancement fits with editing

Enhancement isn't "fix first, then edit" or "edit first, then fix." Both happen inside the multi-track editor: you can trim, denoise, and tweak vocal frequencies at the same time, then export everything together.

## Live preview

Adjust any parameter and the change is audible immediately - no waiting for an export. What you hear in the preview is what you'll hear after exporting.
