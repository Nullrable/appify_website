## The Complete Guide to Phone-Photo-to-PDF

Turning photos into a PDF is useful in more situations than you might think: submitting expense receipts, sending scanned contracts, organizing copies of ID documents, archiving class notes. With Image to PDF, the whole flow happens on your phone in three steps — pick photos, order them, export.

## Step 1: Pick the Right Image Source

The App gives you two ways to pick images:

- **From Photos**: Best for photos you've taken on your phone. Multi-select supported, and you go straight to ordering once you've picked.
- **From Files**: Best for images already saved to iCloud Drive or a local folder.

For formats, the App supports JPG, PNG, HEIC/HEIF, WebP, BMP, and GIF. HEIC is worth calling out — it's iPhone's default capture format, and many online converters don't support it, or they require an extra upload step just to handle it. Here it works directly, so you skip the "convert format first, then convert to PDF" middle step.

## Step 2: Get the Page Order Right

When multiple images become one PDF, page order is reading order. Picked images are laid out as a grid in the App, and you can long-press and drag to reorder. Before exporting, give it one pass for the actual use case:

- **Expense receipts**: order by date so they're easy to verify
- **Contract materials**: main document first, attachments after
- **ID documents**: front side first, back side after

## Step 3: Export and Share

After tapping convert, each PDF page takes its size from the original image — no scaling, no margins added, so the original clarity is preserved end-to-end. The export goes through the system share sheet: save to Files, send via Mail, AirDrop to a colleague nearby — all work. The result also lands in your local history, so if you ever need to send it again, you can grab it straight from history.

## Why "Local Processing" Matters

Most online image-to-PDF tools upload your images to a server for processing. That means your receipts, contracts, and ID photos pass through a server you don't control. Every conversion in Image to PDF runs locally on your device, and files never leave your phone — for sensitive materials, that difference matters more than conversion speed.

If you handle this kind of file often, consider chaining convert → merge → encrypt: first convert to PDF, then use Merge to combine multiple documents, and finally Encrypt to add a password before sending.
