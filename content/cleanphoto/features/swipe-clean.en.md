## Swipe Clean by Month

The scanners find what is repetitive — duplicates, bursts, screenshots. The Swipe view handles everything else: the ordinary months of ordinary photos, where only you can judge what still matters.

### Months as sections

The view slices your library by year and month, newest first. Each month appears as its own section with its photos laid out, so a session has natural boundaries: one month, one sitting, whenever you feel like it.

### Tap into the swipe review

Tap any photo to open the swipe review. There, photos pass by one at a time, and you decide with a swipe: keep or delete. Photos you leave unmarked are untouched — nothing is preselected, and nothing is deleted without your explicit confirmation through the system photo library.

### Filter what you see

A media filter lets you focus the session: all items, photos only, or videos only. That turns the same monthly view into three different tools — a full sweep, a photo-only pass, or a video-only purge.

### Every decision is yours

Unlike the duplicate and screenshot scans, the Swipe view makes no assumptions. It does not know a photo is "bad" — it just puts you in front of your own history, one photo at a time, and lets you keep or let go.

### A gentle way to shrink a library

Month-sized sessions keep the work bounded. Ten minutes on the commute, one month at a time, and a library that grew for years starts shrinking on a schedule you control — with the photos you actually love still exactly where they were.
