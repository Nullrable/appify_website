## How to Clean Up Videos Without Losing the Ones That Matter

A typical phone library is mostly photos, but the storage pressure is mostly videos. A handful of clips — a school concert, a wedding toast, a 4K sunset — can outweigh everything else combined, and the ones that eat the most space are rarely the ones you open a second time. Cleaning them well is less about deleting and more about being honest about which clips you will actually rewatch.

### Sort by month to make sessions bounded

The first move is to break the library into time-bounded sessions — one month, one event, one trip. When the work has a clear start and finish, you decide differently than when you face an undifferentiated list of three hundred clips. Each month is its own review, with its own natural stopping point.

### Show the size, not just the thumbnail

A 30-second clip at 1080p is one thing; the same minute at 4K is another. File size turns the abstract idea of "a video" into a concrete cost you can compare against the memory attached to it. A clip that takes 1.2 GB and will not be rewatched is a clear candidate; a clip that takes 80 MB and captures a moment you re-watch every birthday is not.

### Keep the memory, not the file

The honest question is not "is this video important?" — it is "will I actually open this clip again?" Most clips answer no. The few that answer yes are usually obvious: the ones that come up in conversation, the ones you show people when they ask about something, the ones tied to a specific recurring event. Everything else is storage waiting to be reclaimed.

### Re-watchable clips belong together

Once you have cleared the obvious candidates, the remaining library is small enough to manage as a whole. A short "videos I actually re-watch" folder — or just the unedited library if it is now under a few gigabytes — is the kind of library you stop worrying about.

### Deletion still needs your confirmation

Every deletion goes through the system photo library with your explicit confirmation. iOS and Android each add their own protected deletion prompts, so a stray tap never removes a clip you meant to keep. No video, no frame, and no metadata ever leaves your device in the process.

### A weekly five minutes

Five minutes a week is enough. Open the latest month, look at the largest two or three videos, delete what you know you will not rewatch, and close the app. The library stays bounded, and storage pressure stays out of the way of the things you actually want to keep.
