## Why You Have Duplicate Contacts, and How to Merge Them

Most people who look at their contacts for the first time in years find the same thing: the same friend saved three times, the same colleague split across iCloud and Google, the same family member with an out-of-date address next to a current one. Duplicates do not arrive all at once; they accumulate quietly across backups, imports and platform switches.

### Where duplicates come from

The most common sources are account overlap and number updates. You save someone on iCloud, then switch to Google for a season, then come back to iCloud with both sets merged. You add a friend's new number without deleting the old one. You import a contact from a SIM card or a backup that overlaps with your live address book. Each event is harmless on its own; together they slowly bloat the list.

### Why manual cleanup does not stick

Going through a long address book by hand rarely works. The list is too big, the differences between two entries for the same person are subtle (a middle initial here, a slightly different number formatting there), and the work is never finished — new duplicates arrive faster than you can resolve them by hand. The cleanup that lasts is the one you do once, deliberately, with a tool that surfaces every group at once.

### What "duplicate" actually means

Two contacts are duplicates when they refer to the same person. The most reliable signal is shared identifying information: the same name with the same phone number, or two entries with the same number but slightly different name spellings. A scan that compares on those fields — not on strict string equality — catches the duplicates that manual review usually misses.

### Merging preserves fields, not just names

The goal of a merge is not to keep one entry and discard the other. It is to keep the fullest entry and combine the fields across all duplicates. If one card has a home address and the other has a work email, the merged card should carry both. Cleanup that loses information is worse than the duplicates were.

### Review each group, confirm each merge

There is no safe way to merge automatically without your involvement. Each group should list every duplicate with the fields it contains, so you can see at a glance which entry to keep and which fields to carry over. The merge only happens after you confirm it for that specific group.

### Permission and privacy

Reading your contacts requires the standard system permission, which you grant once. The scan runs locally on your device, contact data never leaves your phone, and there is no cloud copy made by the app. The merged contact is written back to the system address book you already use, so your other apps see the cleaned-up list without any change on their end.
