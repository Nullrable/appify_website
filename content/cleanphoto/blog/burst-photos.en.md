## Why Your Library Is Full of Bursts (and How to Clean Them)

Every good photo has a backstory of near-misses. You press the shutter five times to catch a moving subject, keep the one where everything aligned, and the other four stay in your library for years. Multiply that by every pet, toddler, birthday cake and sunset, and bursts quietly become one of the biggest space-eaters in any photo library.

### Why we shoot in bursts

Bursts are rational. Subjects move, focus hunts, expressions change in a fraction of a second. Shooting a quick series and choosing later is the safest way to get one good frame. The habit is not the problem — the leftovers are. Very few people ever go back to delete the rejects, because reviewing hundreds of look-alike photos by hand is exactly the kind of work we avoid.

### The 10-second rule

This is where CleanPhoto's similar-photo scan helps. It sorts your library by capture time and chains photos together: as long as each photo was taken within 10 seconds of the previous one, it joins the same group. When the gap exceeds 10 seconds, the chain closes. That matches how bursts and rapid retakes actually happen — a quick series within seconds, then a pause, then a different scene. Groups containing a single photo are dropped, so what is left for review is exactly the material worth a decision.

### Reviewing a set, not a pixel war

Each group opens with every photo's file size already loaded, and items are sorted by size. For photos of the same moment, the largest file is almost always the copy that retained the most detail — highest resolution, least compression. So the recommended keeper sits at the top. Check it, confirm it looks right, mark the rest for deletion. The decision shrinks from "which of these six is best?" to "is this one good?" — and that takes a second, not a minute.

### After the event, not months later

The best moment to clean bursts is right after they happen: a holiday dinner, a sports afternoon, a weekend trip. The scan takes a few minutes, you still remember which shots mattered, and the backlog never builds. But even years of accumulated retakes can be cleared the same way — group by group, on the train, in a queue, whenever you have a few spare minutes.

### The usual guarantees

Everything happens on your device. The grouping is based on capture timing only — no content analysis, no pixel reading, no uploads, no account. Deletions run through the system photo library and only after your explicit confirmation. Empty groups disappear automatically, leaving your library exactly as clean as you decided to make it.
