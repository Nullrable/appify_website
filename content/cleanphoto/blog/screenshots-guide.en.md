## The Screenshot Backlog: A Practical Cleanup Guide

Open your photo library and count the screenshots. Not the ones from this week — the ones from 2023. Boarding passes for flights already taken, receipts already expensed, coupons already expired, Wi-Fi passwords already forgotten. For most people, old screenshots are the single biggest pile of photos they will never look at again.

### Why they accumulate

Screenshots are the scratch paper of the phone. You take one to remember something for five minutes, and it lands in the same library as your wedding photos. Nothing in the system ever suggests deleting them, and there is always something newer to scroll to. So they stay — full-screen, rarely edited, and collectively enormous.

### The fastest storage win

This is what makes screenshots the easiest cleanup target: almost none of them matter after a week. A duplicate photo might still be a memory; an old screenshot is almost never one. Clearing them is usually the fastest storage recovery in the whole library, and because the value of each item is close to zero, the review goes quickly.

### How CleanPhoto finds them

On iOS, the system keeps a dedicated Screenshots album, and CleanPhoto reads it directly — detection is exact. On Android, where storage varies by manufacturer, the scan checks album names first and, when no screenshot album exists, inspects each image's title and file path for the naming patterns devices use. Everything found is grouped by the day it was captured, newest first, with each item's file size shown.

### A workflow that works

Start from the newest day and work backwards — recent screenshots are the ones you are most likely to still need, so the easy decisions come first. Keep the rare keepers (a warranty code, a saved conversation), mark the rest, confirm. Or take the pragmatic route: everything older than three months goes, and you stop right there. Because groups are ordered by day, you can halt at any boundary without touching the rest.

### Two small habits

First, when you take a screenshot for a genuinely temporary purpose — a code, a map, a price — clean it the same week. Second, run the screenshot scan after any travel or shopping period, which is when the pile grows fastest. Five minutes, done.

### Nothing leaves your phone

Screenshot detection reads only album names, titles and file paths on your device. Nothing is uploaded, no account is needed, and deletions happen only through your explicit confirmation via the system photo library.
