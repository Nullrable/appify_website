## Ultra-wide capture: fit A3 and bound books in a single frame

The standard 1× camera on most phones covers about 70° of field of view. Paper Scan's ultra-wide lens extends this to roughly 120°, letting you capture full pages from a normal seated position. Hold the phone 30 cm above an A4 page and the entire page fills the frame; the same distance works for A3 handouts, whiteboards and most bound materials.

Edge detection, perspective correction and shadow removal run the moment the capture button is pressed, producing a flat page rather than a photograph of paper.

### When ultra-wide helps

- **Large flat documents.** A3 meeting handouts, folded maps, restaurant menus, magazine spreads and posters. The wider field of view captures the page without standing up or stepping back.
- **Bound books and manuals.** Stiff spines force the page into a slight curve near the binding, which costs text in the inner margin. Holding the phone slightly further away reduces the curve optically before any software correction runs.
- **Whiteboards and presentation screens.** Capture the full board or screen from a seated position in a classroom or meeting room. Perspective correction handles the keystone effect of viewing the surface at an angle.
- **Restricted workspace.** When the surrounding area is occupied with coffee cups, hands or other documents, the wider field of view captures the page without needing physical room to step back.

### When to use the 1× lens instead

The ultra-wide lens resolves less detail per square centimetre, since the same image sensor is spread across a wider area. Use the standard 1× lens for:

- **Dense small text.** Contracts, legal documents, footnotes, fine print. Corner sharpness matters more than field of view.
- **Glossy media.** Laminated IDs, magazine covers, plastic-coated surfaces. The wider aperture gathers more reflection at the edges.
- **Low-light conditions.** The ultra-wide lens typically has a smaller maximum aperture and produces more noise in dim environments.

The lens toggle is a single tap in the viewfinder.

### Shooting best practices

- **Even lighting.** Indirect light from a window performs better than a single overhead spotlight. Shadows crossing the page are the most common cause of blur and OCR misreads.
- **Stable hands.** Rest elbows on the table. The shutter waits for a stable frame before firing, so a brief pause usually replaces a re-scan.
- **Correct focus.** If auto-focus selects the wrong subject (the desk, a finger, the page edge), tap the centre of the text before capture.
- **Flat pages.** For bound documents, press the page flat with the other hand. Pages that bow toward the spine lose characters near the gutter.

The ultra-wide lens requires iPhone 11 or newer, or a flagship Android device released in the last several years. Devices without an ultra-wide camera fall back to the 1× lens automatically; the rest of the capture pipeline is unaffected.
