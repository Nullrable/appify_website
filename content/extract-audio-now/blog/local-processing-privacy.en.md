## Why local processing beats uploading to the cloud

A lot of online video tools follow the same flow: you upload, the server processes, you download. Three steps, sounds simple. But for personal users, that path has three real downsides.

## Your video leaves your device

The moment you upload, your video is sitting on someone else's server. Even if it's deleted right after processing, it existed there, was processed there, and may have left a trace in some log. Family footage, business meetings, interview material, personal projects - once content leaves your device, it isn't only yours anymore.

In Extract Audio, every operation runs on your iPhone. The video file stays in your library from start to finish - it never leaves.

## You wait in line for the server

Cloud tools need to queue jobs. Free users wait, paid users cut the line - which means your video might be processed alongside someone else's, and speed depends on how busy the server is. Local processing has no such problem. The encoder is on your device, and the queue length is always one.

## Upload and download both eat data

A 4K, one-minute video can easily hit 500 MB. Upload 500 MB, download the processed 500 MB - that's 1 GB of data right there. Local processing only writes the new file to your library: zero data usage. In a cafe, on the subway, in a dead spot - local tools keep working.

## When cloud actually makes sense

If your video is professional footage in the tens of GB, or you need effects only a cloud pipeline can run, local tools hit their limit. Extract Audio is built for everyday personal use - videos in your phone's library, lecture screen recordings, clips from family and friends - and those scenarios are exactly where local processing wins.

## "Local" isn't a marketing slogan

"Local processing" can sound like ad copy, but for you it's literal: the file never left the device, so there's nothing to leak or misuse. The day you happen to be processing a sensitive business meeting or a personal project you haven't published yet, that matters.
