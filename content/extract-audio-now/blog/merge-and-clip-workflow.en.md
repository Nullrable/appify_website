## Merge + Clip workflow

Merge and Clip are two separate tools, but they tend to chain together in real use - especially for long-form material. This guide walks through one common flow: merge several clips first, then clip down the result to what you actually want.

## Why merge first, clip second

The natural-sounding order is "clip each piece, then merge them." In practice, the reverse is faster.

**Per-piece clipping is painful:**

- You set start/end points over and over - repeated work
- Pieces may not "snap together," leaving gaps between them
- The overall pacing only becomes clear after you've merged them

**Merge-then-clip wins because:**

- You see the whole thing in one go and judge pacing better
- Joins between clips are obvious once merged, so it's a drag to see what to keep
- You skip the per-clip setup overhead

## A typical workflow

**Scenario: three family videos, want a 5-minute highlight reel**

1. Open Merge, pick the three videos, drag them into time order
2. Wait for the merge to finish - you now have one long video
3. Open Clip, drag both handles, keep the parts you want
4. If you're not satisfied, merge again with different combinations and try another cut

**Scenario: a 90-minute lecture, keep only the 30-minute core**

1. The video is one continuous recording - no merge needed, just Clip
2. Drag the in-point and out-point, skip the intros and small talk
3. Export

You won't always need Merge, but when your chain is "many pieces -> one piece -> take a slice of it," Merge + Clip is the natural combo.

## Other useful combos

**Merge -> Speed**

Different-length clips merged together often have uneven pacing. Merge first, then apply a uniform speed change - slow down or speed up the whole thing so the rhythm is consistent.

**Clip -> Compress**

A clipped video often still has room to shrink. Clip first, then compress - a good final step before sharing.

**Merge -> GIF**

Stitch a few highlights together, then grab a few seconds as a GIF - perfect for reaction memes and social sharing.

## The order can flip

The tools don't force a sequence. Merge-then-clip is "rough cut first, refine after." Clip-then-merge is "fine cut first, combine after," for when each piece needs precise editing on its own. Both are reasonable - pick by your material and your end goal.

## A simple rule of thumb

If your goal is "end up with one video," merge-then-clip is usually faster. If your goal is "each piece becomes its own short video," clip them individually.
